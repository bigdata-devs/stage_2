Microbenchmarks of the Indexing Service text pipeline
======================================================

Command (repository root, MongoDB not needed):

    go test ./internal/indexer -run '^$' -bench . -benchmem -cpu 1,2,4,8 -benchtime=500ms

Corpus: the 20 real Project Gutenberg books stored under datalake/ (9,829,441 body bytes),
loaded once before the timer starts. Every benchmark cycles the whole corpus, with one corpus
cursor per goroutine, so each -cpu value measures aggregate throughput and not shared state
contention. SetBytes reports the average book size: body for ProcessText, header for
ExtractMetadata, and the reconstructed raw plain-text book for SplitBookText (the datalake keeps
header and body already split, so the raw layout is rebuilt around the real content).

Reading of the numbers
----------------------

- ProcessText (tokenize + stop-word/numeral filtering) is the dominant per-book CPU cost:
  about 21.6 ms per average book on one core, dropping to 5.2 ms with 8 workers (4.1x), and
  ~10.4 MB of allocations per book, which is why the rebuild streams one book at a time.
- ExtractMetadata (header parsing) is negligible: ~64 us on one core, ~11 us at 8 workers.
- SplitBookText (Stage 1 marker split + trim) is I/O-bound in practice: ~0.9 ms on one core,
  zero allocations, ~4.2 GB/s at 8 workers; the ingestion path is not the bottleneck.
